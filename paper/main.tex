\title{\bfseries When Does INT8 Actually Accelerate Edge Inference?\\[0.45em]
\large A Reproducible Audit of MobileNetV2 Conversion and Packaging}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jaiveer Bassi\,\orcidicon}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Independent Researcher, USA\\
\texttt{jaiveerbassi@yahoo.com}\\
{\footnotesize ORCID: 0009-0006-3633-3220}}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Eight-bit quantization is often presented as a direct route to smaller and faster edge models, but bit width alone does not determine realized latency. We audit a reproducible MobileNetV2 TensorFlow Lite pipeline across quantization granularity, CPU delegate, thread count, batch size, process restarts, and container serving. On the complete 3,925-image Imagenette validation split, per-channel INT8 reduced storage by 71.5\% and changed top-1 accuracy by $-0.92$ percentage points (paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[-1.66,-0.18]$; exact McNemar $p=.0165$). Across five fresh processes on an AMD Ryzen 5 7600, one-thread XNNPACK produced a 1.43$\times$ paired geometric-mean speedup (95\% CI $[1.18,1.73]$), whereas built-in kernels made INT8 2.22$\times$ slower (FP32/INT8 speedup 0.45$\times$, 95\% CI $[0.40,0.51]$). Per-tensor INT8 had similar latency to per-channel INT8 but collapsed to 1.04\% top-1 accuracy. The packaged service occupied 339.58 MiB uncompressed, reached health in a median 1.69 s, used 77.36 MiB resident memory after load, and delivered localhost HTTP p50/p99 latency of 46.34/71.85 ms. The central finding is that backend choice and quantization granularity govern whether INT8 is both accurate and fast.
\end{abstract}
