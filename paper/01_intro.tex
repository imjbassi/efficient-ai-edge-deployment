\begin{abstract}
The deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) models on resource-constrained edge devices demands aggressive optimization. While existing solutions often rely on disjointed compression techniques, this paper introduces a hardware-aware pipeline that integrates quantization, pruning, and lightweight containerization using specialized runtime frameworks. Unlike standard industry tools such as TensorFlow Lite or NVIDIA TensorRT—which primarily apply static, post-training optimizations—our methodology jointly adapts model architecture and precision while actively managing memory migration. We evaluate our pipeline on both vision and language models across embedded GPU boards and low-power CPUs. Empirical results demonstrate that our optimized MobileNet deployment achieves real-time inference on a Raspberry Pi-class device, delivering a 4x reduction in memory footprint and a 50\% to 70\% increase in inference throughput. Crucially, these efficiency gains are achieved with a strictly bounded impact on performance, limiting Top-1 accuracy degradation to exactly 1.2\% on the ImageNet validation set compared to the unoptimized baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has led to models of unprecedented scale and capability. However, deploying these models on edge devices—such as IoT sensors, mobile phones, and embedded autonomous systems—presents significant challenges. Edge environments are characterized by strict constraints on compute, memory, and power. Standard cloud-based deployment paradigms are therefore unsuitable, necessitating highly efficient packaging and inference techniques.

While pruning and quantization are well-established methods for model compression, current workflows treat model optimization and containerized deployment as separate steps. This disconnect often leads to suboptimal hardware utilization. In this paper, we propose an integrated approach to efficient AI edge deployment. By co-optimizing the model compression phase with the specific constraints of the target containerized runtime, we ensure that models are not just theoretically smaller, but practically faster and more energy-efficient on the actual target hardware.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 details our unified methodology for pruning, quantization, and runtime containerization. Section 3 presents a rigorous empirical evaluation against industry-standard baselines, focusing on latency, accuracy, and thermal efficiency. Finally, Section 4 concludes with a discussion on the broader implications for edge AI.
