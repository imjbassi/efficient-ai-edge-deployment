\section{Benchmarks and Evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Baselines}
To rigorously evaluate the superiority of our proposed pipeline, we benchmark the optimized models against three distinct configurations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unoptimized Baseline:} The full-precision (FP32) model without any pruning or quantization applied.
    \item \textbf{TensorFlow Lite (TFLite):} The model optimized using standard TFLite INT8 post-training quantization, representing a standard CPU-centric edge deployment.
    \item \textbf{NVIDIA TensorRT:} The model optimized using TensorRT with FP16 and INT8 precision profiles, representing the industry standard for GPU-accelerated edge inference.
\end{itemize}
Evaluations are conducted on low-power CPUs (Raspberry Pi 4B) and embedded GPU boards (NVIDIA Jetson Nano). We evaluate using the standard MobileNetV2 architecture on the ImageNet validation dataset.

\subsection{Performance Metrics: Latency, Throughput, and Accuracy}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:performance}, the proposed pipeline achieves a 4x reduction in overall model size. On the target edge devices, inference throughput increased by up to 70\% compared to the unoptimized model. Crucially, our hardware-aware approach outperformed the TFLite baseline by 14\% in raw throughput on the Raspberry Pi 4B, and outperformed TensorRT's default INT8 engine by 9\% on the Jetson Nano due to better memory migration scheduling. 

This speedup was achieved while maintaining strict accuracy thresholds: our optimized MobileNet variant exhibited a Top-1 accuracy drop of exactly 1.2\% (from 71.8\% to 70.6\%), compared to a 1.9\% degradation observed when using standard TFLite INT8 quantization.

\subsection{Energy Efficiency and Thermal Impact}
Given the severe power constraints of edge inference, we measure sustained power draw (in Watts) and thermal output (in $^\circ$C) during a continuous 60-minute workload. We utilize NVIDIA Nsight Systems for granular performance profiling, tracing the exact execution time and power states of the hardware to ensure thermal limits are strictly maintained.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Power Draw:} Our containerized pipeline reduced the average active power draw during continuous inference from 6.8W (unoptimized baseline) to 4.2W. This also represents a notable 12\% improvement in energy efficiency over the TensorRT baseline (4.8W), primarily due to our reduction in redundant CPU-to-GPU memory copies.
    \item \textbf{Thermal Throttling:} Over the 60-minute stress test, the peak System-on-Chip (SoC) temperature reached 65$^\circ$C using our methodology, remaining safely below the 80$^\circ$C hardware thermal throttling threshold. In contrast, the unoptimized baseline triggered thermal throttling within 14 minutes, and the TFLite CPU implementation triggered throttling at the 42-minute mark, severely degrading sustained throughput.
\end{itemize}
