\subsection{Accuracy depends on granularity}

Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} shows that per-channel INT8 retained most FP32 accuracy while reducing storage from 13.34 to 3.81 MiB (71.5\%). The top-1 comparison is not merely two overlapping marginal intervals: among the same 3,925 images, 125 were correct only for FP32 and 89 only for INT8. The resulting $-0.92$ percentage-point difference has paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[-1.66,-0.18]$ and exact McNemar $p=.0165$. Top-5 changed by $-0.94$ points (76 FP32-only versus 39 INT8-only; CI $[-1.48,-0.41]$; $p=.00072$).

The per-tensor control was 10.2\% smaller than per-channel INT8 but achieved only 1.04\% top-1 and 3.95\% top-5 accuracy. Its one-thread XNNPACK latency (7.35 ms; 95\% CI $[7.00,7.69]$) was close to per-channel INT8. Yet it predicted only 187 of 1,000 classes across 3,925 images, placed 19.6\% of predictions in one class, and had prediction entropy 5.11 bits versus a 9.97-bit maximum. In the valid per-channel graph, 17 depthwise weight tensors had within-layer scale ratios as high as 742$\times$ (channel-range ratio 754$\times$). The same evaluator and preprocessing produce normal per-channel accuracy, supporting numerical granularity---not an evaluation bug---as the cause of collapse. Quantization granularity is therefore part of model correctness, not a packaging detail.

\subsection{Speedup depends on the execution backend}

The one-thread XNNPACK condition is the primary latency comparison. FP32 and per-channel INT8 mean-of-run-means were 9.48 ms (95\% CI $[8.93,10.02]$) and 7.43 ms ($[7.14,7.71]$), respectively. Matched run ratios yield a 1.27$\times$ geometric-mean speedup ($[1.20,1.35]$). The pooled empirical distributions in Fig.~\ref{fig:latency} show substantial jitter; inference-level samples characterize tails, while the interval is deliberately computed across process-level means.

Table~\ref{tab:latency} demonstrates that this result does not extrapolate to a universal multiplier. XNNPACK speedups range from 1.22$\times$ to 1.27$\times$ across thread counts; all paired intervals exclude unity after 20 restarts. Batch four increases the paired speedup to 1.38$\times$ but does not materially reduce FP32 per-image latency. Most importantly, disabling default delegates reverses the result: built-in FP32 averages 34.59 ms and built-in INT8 averages 77.27 ms, making INT8 2.24$\times$ slower. Thus, the optimized kernel path---not the file's nominal bit width---determines whether the conversion accelerates inference. This observation is consistent with benchmark literature that treats software stacks and hardware as first-class experimental variables \cite{reddi2020mlperf,reddi2020mobile,banbury2021mlperf}.

\subsection{Packaging is measurable}

The slim production image is 339.58 MiB uncompressed---89.2$\times$ its 3.81 MiB model---and 81.43 MiB as a compressed image archive. The standard-base control is 1,543.65 MiB uncompressed and 393.44 MiB compressed, so the slim base reduces the two measures by 78.0\% and 79.3\%. Across 20 clean starts, median time from Compose invocation to the first successful health response is 1.52 s (range 1.39--1.83 s); ready-state RSS is 65.05--65.72 MiB.

After warm-up, 200 sequential localhost requests have mean 51.97 ms, p50 49.87 ms, p90 65.80 ms, and p99 73.96 ms. The additive mean decomposition is 38.66 ms for container invocation, 10.96 ms for client/loopback transport, and 2.35 ms for all other server work. The latter comprises framework/multipart parsing (0.67 ms), decode (0.30 ms), resize/crop (0.74 ms), normalize/quantize (0.26 ms), postprocessing (0.11 ms), serialization (0.04 ms), and 0.23 ms unattributed; upload read and queue wait round below 0.01 ms. Thus 31.23 ms of the 44.54 ms gap from 7.43 ms native to 51.97 ms HTTP mean occurs inside the container's invocation boundary, while measured transport contributes 10.96 ms. Runtime/version, virtualization, and the two-CPU ceiling remain bundled within that invocation difference. RSS after requests is 77.16 MiB.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

The paired accuracy inference applies to this fixed 3,925-image subset; Imagenette does not replace full ImageNet evaluation. Latency was measured on one x86-64 workstation host, not ARM, a neural accelerator, or a battery-powered target. The deliberately interactive host condition and sampled pre-run CPU load differ from an idle laboratory benchmark, although 20 independent starts and paired intervals expose rather than hide that variation. The Windows-hosted Docker measurement includes virtualization and loopback transport and should not be generalized to native Linux. Energy, temperature, concurrent-request throughput, and multi-model generality remain unmeasured.
